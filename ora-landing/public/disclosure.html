Ora — Disclosure, Terms of Use, and Privacy Notice

Effective: August 17, 2026. Version 1.0.

This page combines Ora's public disclosure statement, terms of use, and privacy notice. It is the load-bearing legal document for the app until Ora is separately insured. Before you use Ora, please read it. Using Ora means you agree to it.

This document is not legal advice. Consult your own attorney for how recording laws apply to your specific situation.

1. What Ora Is

Ora is an iPhone app that records audio you initiate, transcribes it on your device, and generates a written summary. Ora is designed as a personal tool for the individual user of the device it is installed on.

Ora is not:

  • A team collaboration tool
  • A CRM integration
  • A service that joins your video calls as a participant
  • A legal, compliance, or consent-management product

Ora is a recording and note-taking utility. Nothing more.

2. Who Is Responsible for Recording — You

You, the user of the device, are solely responsible for:

  1. Deciding when to start a recording.
  2. Obtaining any consent required by the laws of your jurisdiction and the jurisdictions of every other participant in the recorded conversation.
  3. Complying with the terms of the platforms (Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, phone carrier, etc.) that you are using alongside Ora.
  4. Storing, sharing, and deleting your recordings and transcripts appropriately.
  5. Not using Ora to record conversations you are not authorized to record.

Ora does not obtain consent on your behalf. Ora does not verify consent. Ora does not warn other participants that they are being recorded. Those responsibilities are yours.

3. Recording Laws Are Real and Vary by Jurisdiction

Recording laws differ significantly by state and country. Some jurisdictions (including the United States federal baseline under 18 U.S.C. § 2511) require only one party to the conversation to consent — meaning you can generally record a conversation you are part of.

Other jurisdictions require every participant to consent. These include (in the United States) California, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, and Washington. Michigan's rule is disputed and is safest to treat as all-party. See the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press guide for current details.

In several of these jurisdictions, recording without required consent can carry serious civil and criminal penalties. California's Invasion of Privacy Act (Cal. Penal Code § 631) allows for statutory damages per violation. Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act (740 ILCS 14) has its own regime for voice biometrics. The federal Electronic Communications Privacy Act may apply.

Before you record any conversation using Ora, you are responsible for knowing:

  • Which jurisdiction each participant is in
  • What that jurisdiction's consent rules require
  • Whether you have obtained the required consent
  • How you have documented that consent

If you are uncertain, do not record. Or announce your recording at the start of the conversation and get affirmative agreement from every participant.

4. What Ora Does With Your Data

Ora is designed to keep your data on your device by default. Here is exactly what happens:

Audio recording:

  • Captured by your iPhone's microphone.
  • Saved as an .m4a file in Ora's local Documents folder on your device.
  • Not uploaded to Ora servers.
  • Not transmitted to any third party by Ora.

Transcription:

  • Performed on your iPhone using Apple's built-in SFSpeechRecognizer framework, subject to Apple's Speech Recognition privacy disclosures.
  • The transcript is text produced from your audio on your device.

Summarization (recap generation):

  • The text transcript only — never the audio — is sent to a third-party language model API (currently Groq, subject to their terms and privacy policy) to generate a written recap.
  • The audio file itself is never transmitted.
  • The transcript text is sent over an encrypted connection. Ora does not retain a copy of the transmitted transcript on Ora servers; the round trip is generation-only.
  • The third-party provider's data handling is governed by their own terms. Ora does not control it.

Local storage:

  • Recordings, transcripts, and recaps live in Ora's local storage on your device.
  • If you have iCloud Backup enabled for the Ora app, they may be included in your iCloud backup. See Apple's iCloud privacy documentation for how iCloud data is handled.
  • Ora does not run a cloud service to store your recordings.

Analytics and marketing:

  • Ora's public website (meetora.app) uses first-party analytics that record page views, referring source (UTM parameters), and email captures for the waitlist.
  • The Ora app itself may log anonymized, aggregate usage events (feature taps, crash reports) to help us improve the product. It does not log the content of your recordings or transcripts.
  • If you opt in to marketing emails, we use MailerLite to deliver them, subject to MailerLite's privacy policy.

We do not:

  • Sell your data to third parties.
  • Use your recordings or transcripts to train AI models.
  • Share your recordings with anyone.

5. Comparison to Other Meeting Tools

Ora is architected differently from meeting notetakers that operate by having a bot join your video calls. Here is the difference in plain terms:

Bot-based notetakers:

  • A named third-party participant joins your call.
  • Audio is captured and uploaded to the vendor's cloud infrastructure.
  • Transcription and summarization happen on the vendor's servers.
  • The vendor's data handling policies govern what happens to your audio.

Ora:

  • No third party joins your call.
  • You initiate the recording on your own device.
  • Audio never leaves your iPhone.
  • Only the transcript text is briefly transmitted for summary generation.

We believe this architecture is meaningfully more private for the user. We do not claim it exempts you from any recording law. It doesn't. See Section 3.
