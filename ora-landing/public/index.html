Questions people actually ask

FAQ

Is my audio really staying on my iPhone?

Yes. The recording is captured by the iPhone microphone and saved as an .m4a file in Ora's local Documents folder. It's not uploaded to Ora servers. Transcription happens on your device using Apple's built-in SFSpeechRecognizer. Only the text transcript — never the audio — is briefly sent to a language model API to generate the recap. Full details in our Disclosure page.

What states require me to tell people I'm recording?

12 states are "all-party consent" states — California, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington, and Michigan is best treated as one. The federal baseline is one-party consent (you can record a conversation you're part of). Ora doesn't obtain consent for you — that's your responsibility. Page 11 of the free playbook is a state-by-state cheat sheet.

Will it record if I get a phone call mid-meeting?

Yes, with the right setting. Settings → Focus → Do Not Disturb → "Silence Notifications: Always." Fix takes 15 seconds. Page 4 of the playbook walks through it.

Does Ora work for Zoom/Google Meet/Teams?

Yes — put your phone on the desk near your laptop, or use the speaker output. Ora hears whatever your iPhone's mic hears. It's also the only meeting notetaker that works in coffee shops, on walks, and at in-person site visits.

Is there an Android version?

No. Ora is iPhone-only. Building for both platforms at once is how you ship a mediocre product for both. We picked iPhone.

What happens if I hit some kind of monthly limit?

You don't. The Annual plan has no monthly minute cap, no per-meeting length cap, and no artificial throttling. If Otter's 1,200 minutes ran out at meeting 11 of the month, that's the pain we're solving.

Is Ora legal to use everywhere?

Ora is a recording tool. Whether recording a specific conversation is legal is up to you and depends on your jurisdiction and the jurisdictions of every participant. We're not lawyers. Read our Disclosure & Terms and the state-by-state cheat sheet in the playbook.
