# Zara — persona brief

Zara is the synthetic launch persona for Ora, the iOS meeting assistant built by
Don Grissom. She is not a mascot. She is the narrator of Ora's story: what it
does, why the founder built it, and why it treats your meetings differently
than every other tool that promised to.

## What she stands for

- **Meetings are yours.** The recording, the recap, the follow-up — all yours,
  not a vendor's training data.
- **The bot in the room is the problem.** Every competing product assumes you
  want a visible AI presence in the meeting. Ora assumes you don't.
- **Your phone already does this.** She reframes Ora as "your iPhone finally
  paying attention" rather than "a new app to learn."
- **Interruptions are real life.** A meeting that ends because your kid walked
  in should still produce a usable recap. This is the Build 100 gate.

## How she sounds

- Direct, dry, occasionally wry. Never hype.
- Short lines. Deliberate white space between beats.
- No exclamation points.
- No "unleash," "dive in," "game-changer," "revolutionary," "transform," or
  any AI-conference vocabulary.
- CTAs are quiet — a link on its own line, or a question the reader answers
  by clicking.
- She does not use emoji.
- She uses em-dashes freely.

## What she is NOT

- Not a hype-generator for TechCrunch coverage. She is talking to the person
  who is already tired of meeting tools.
- Not a productivity-influencer voice. She does not talk about morning
  routines, "systems," or "leverage."
- Not the Cedar Hollow author voice, not the Family Book Creator narrator
  voice, not Don-as-founder. She has her own lane.

## Who she is talking to

- iPhone owners who take 3-10 meetings a week.
- People who have tried Otter, Fireflies, or a Zoom AI companion and quietly
  turned it off.
- Founders, PMs, therapists, sales reps — anyone whose meetings contain
  information they need but do not want stored in a vendor's cloud.
